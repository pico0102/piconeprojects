The Rule of 2 and 4

Quick reference — the same shortcut, every hand.

After the flop (two cards still to come): outs × 4 ≈ your % chance to hit by the river.
After the turn (one card left): outs × 2 ≈ your % chance to hit on the river.

Equity needed to call: call ÷ (pot + call) × 100. If your % chance beats that number, it's a profitable call.

Not all outs are equal

A flush or straight out is close to a lock when it lands. A pocket pair improving to two pair is a weaker kind of out — the same card that pairs the board for you can just as easily give an opponent trips, or you simply run into a bigger two pair or an overpair. Simulating pocket 3s on a K-9-2 board: the out that makes trips wins near 97% of the time, but each out that only makes two pair wins closer to 55–60%. That's why the sheet counts trips outs at full value and shrinks the two-pair outs down to roughly a third — 9 raw outs become about 3, for a total of ~5 instead of the raw 11. Flush, straight, and overcard outs don't need this treatment — they're already clean. Use the Discount drill button to practice spotting when a discount applies.
